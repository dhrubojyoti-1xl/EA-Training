The one skill that unlocks all of them05 / 24

Brief the AI the way you'd brief a new colleague.

Four parts, every time: who it is, what's going on, what you need, and what the output should look like.

  1. RRole — “You are an executive assistant to a CEO.”
  2. CContext — what happened, who's involved, what's at stake, what's been tried.
  3. TTask — the one thing you want back.
  4. FFormat — length, structure, tone, language (“British English, under 120 words, subject line included”).
Three habits that double the quality: paste one example of your own writing so it matches your voice · ask for three versions and pick the best · never send the first draft unread.

Weak brief

Write an email to the vendor.

Strong brief

You are an executive assistant to a CEO. Context: the vendor missed the 15 September delivery for the office fit-out; this is their second delay. Task: write a firm but courteous email asking for a revised date within 48 hours and a written explanation. Format: under 120 words, subject line included, British English, sign off as “Office of the CEO”.

Pillar 1 · Implementation & Process Development06 / 24

Become the person who knows how everything works.

From your JD — “Understand software and tools used within the organisation and support their effective implementation across teams. Learn and explore relevant AI tools and guide teams on their effective usage.”

  1. 1Learn any tool in an hour. Drop its help pages or PDF manual into NotebookLM (or Claude) and ask for a 10-step explanation plus the five mistakes beginners make. Then do the steps yourself once.
  2. 2Roll it out properly. Claude drafts the rollout plan: who, when, training format, a one-page quick-start, and how you'll know it worked.
  3. 3Be the AI guide. Keep a one-page “approved AI tools and rules” document, run 20-minute Loom demos per team, and collect the best prompts in a shared prompt library (a Google Doc is enough).
Copy this prompt

I'm rolling out [tool name] to the [Content Writing] team — 6 people, fully remote, mixed confidence with new software. Draft a 2-week rollout plan with: two 30-minute training sessions (agenda for each), a one-page quick-start guide, the three problems people will hit first and how to fix them, and how we will measure that the rollout worked after 30 days.

Measure adoption, not enthusiasm: after 30 days, count who uses the tool weekly, the time saved on one task, and the questions still coming to you.

Pillar 1 · Implementation & Process Development08 / 24

Training videos without a video team.

From your JD — “Prepare and maintain process documents, policies, rules, regulations, and training materials, including videos.”

Loom

Screen + voice · 3-minute how-tos

Record your screen while you do the task; Loom writes the title, summary and chapters. Best for “how to do X” — the video most teams actually need.

Descript

Edit video by editing text

Delete a sentence from the transcript and it's cut from the video. Removes filler words, adds captions, exports clean.

HeyGen / Synthesia

Script → presenter video

Paste a script, choose an avatar, get a polished explainer in minutes — and in other languages. Best for policies and onboarding modules.

Canva + Gamma

Visuals and decks from an outline

Canva for one-pagers, posters and thumbnails; Gamma turns a text outline into a presentable deck you then tidy.

One video, one process, under five minutes. Open with what the viewer will be able to do; end with where the SOP lives. Re-record when the process changes — don't patch.
Policies, rules and regulations: Claude drafts from your bullet points → Gemini in Docs formats and adds a summary box → leadership approves → filed in the Policies folder with a version number and an “effective from” date.
Copy this prompt

Write a 3-minute training video script for [process]. Structure: what you'll be able to do (10 seconds), the steps in order with what's on screen for each, two common mistakes, and a closing line pointing to the SOP. Conversational, second person, no jargon.

Pillar 3 · Coordination & Content Support16 / 24

You are the signal, not the noise.

From your JD — “Ensure smooth communication between departments and leadership.”

  • Upward — the weekly leadership brief. One page, same format every week: Wins · Risks · Decisions needed · Next week. Claude drafts it from the tracker export and the week's minutes; you make it true.
  • Downward — decisions travel within 24 hours. Every leadership decision becomes a short “what changes for you” note to the teams it affects.
  • Chat hygiene. One channel per project; AI summarises long threads at the end of the day; every decision is restated in writing where it was made.
  • Escalation template. What's the issue · impact · what I've tried · what I need · by when. Five lines, no drama.

Trust test: if leadership could read the brief without you in the room and still know exactly what to do, it's finished.

In Sheets · Gemini

Compare Sheet1 (bank statement export) with Sheet2 (expense tracker). List: entries that appear in one but not the other, possible duplicates (same amount within 3 days), and any expense over 25,000 without an invoice number. Put the results in a new tab with a one-line explanation per row.

Where financial data lives: inside company Workspace tools and approved expense software only — never pasted into a consumer chat app. Account numbers, card details and bank logins never go into any AI tool (slide 21).

Pillar 6 · Confidentiality & Professionalism21 / 24

Trust is the job. AI must never cost you it.

From your JD — “Handle sensitive information with the highest level of confidentiality and integrity. Represent leadership professionally in internal and external communication.”

Never paste into any AI toolno exceptions
  • Passwords, logins, one-time codes
  • Bank, card or account numbers
  • ID documents, passports, personal addresses
  • Salaries and personal employee data
  • Unreleased financial results, contracts under negotiation
  • Legal or HR cases; anything marked confidential
Alwaysthe habits that keep you safe
  • Use company-approved accounts — Gemini in Workspace, Claude or ChatGPT Team/Enterprise — which keep data out of model training
  • Anonymise: “the vendor”, “Employee A”, “Project X”
  • Check data-retention and sharing settings once a quarter
  • Keep transcripts and summaries inside the company workspace; ask before recording anyone
Representing leadership: their voice, not yours — keep a tone guide with three sample emails in a Claude Project or Gemini. When in doubt, formal and brief. Never commit leadership to a date or decision by email without checking first.
Core skills · Remote work & communication22 / 24

Remote means written. Make your writing effortless to read.

From your JD — “Excellent written and verbal communication in English (mandatory) … confident in handling responsibilities in a fast-paced, remote work environment … Experience in remote work environments is an added advantage.”

  • Async first. A written update beats a meeting. Daily end-of-day note to the executive: Done · Tomorrow · Blocked · Need. Loom for anything visual.
  • Time zones are stated, never assumed. Both times in every message; scheduling links instead of back-and-forth; protect the executive's deep-work hours.
  • English that lands. Short sentences. One ask per message. Grammarly for correctness; Claude for tone — “confident and warm, British English, under 80 words”. Read it aloud once before sending.
  • Verbal confidence. Rehearse a difficult call with ChatGPT voice mode: “Play a demanding vendor; I'll negotiate a new delivery date.”
  • Presence on camera. Camera on for leadership meetings, agenda shared, close every call with actions and owners.
